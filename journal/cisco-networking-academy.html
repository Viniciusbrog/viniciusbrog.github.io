Starting my networking journey with Cisco.

My notes, discoveries and practical progress as I build a foundation in computer networking and learn how devices communicate.

Networking is one of the foundations I want to understand properly before moving deeper into cybersecurity. Security tools and investigations make much more sense when I understand how devices communicate, how data travels and where problems or vulnerabilities can appear.

I'm using Cisco Networking Academy as one of my learning resources. This page will be my working notebook: I'll update it as I complete lessons, practise concepts and discover questions worth investigating.

MY OBJECTIVEBuild a practical understanding of networking, not just memorise definitions. I want to be able to explain what is happening when information moves across a network.

01. Why networking matters

Almost every connected system depends on networks. Websites, cloud platforms, business applications, computers and security devices all exchange information through network infrastructure.

For a future cybersecurity professional, networking knowledge helps with understanding traffic, investigating suspicious activity, troubleshooting connectivity and recognising how attacks may move through an environment.

02. Concepts I'm studying

Network fundamentals

Understanding what a network is, how devices connect, and the roles of routers, switches, access points and end devices.

IP addressing and subnetting

Learning how devices are identified on a network, how IPv4 addresses are structured, and how subnet masks divide networks into smaller sections.

Protocols and communication

Exploring the purpose of TCP, UDP, DNS, DHCP, HTTP and HTTPS, and how protocols make communication possible.

OSI and TCP/IP models

Using networking models to understand the different stages involved in sending and receiving data, and to organise troubleshooting.

My goal is to pair theory with practice. As I complete exercises, I'll add the task, tools used, what I observed and what I learned.

  • Identify the IP configuration of my computer.

  • Build a small network topology and understand device roles.
  • Capture and inspect network packets with Wireshark when ready.
